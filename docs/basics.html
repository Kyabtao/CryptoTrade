1 · Markets 101

What is actually happening when you “trade crypto”.

A crypto exchange (Binance, Kraken, Coinbase…) is a website where people buy and sell digital assets with each other. A pair like BTC/USDT simply means “Bitcoin priced in Tether (a dollar-pegged coin)” — the bot trades that pair, and its virtual accounts hold either USDT cash or BTC.

An order is an instruction: a market order buys/sells immediately at whatever price is available; a limit order says “only trade at this price or better”, and may wait. This bot places market-style orders at each candle's close, and virtual limit orders for grid strategies.

Why do prices move?
Every trade has a buyer and a seller. When more people want to buy than sell at the current price, buyers raise their bids and price climbs; when sellers outnumber buyers, price falls. “The market” is just this auction — no central planner decides the price.
What is an exchange's order book?
A live list of all resting buy and sell limit orders, with sizes. The best bid/ask at the top is where market orders fill. Liquid markets (like BTC/USDT) have deep books — your order fills almost instantly at a fair price, day or night.
Can I lose more than I put in?
With spot (no leverage) and no shorting: no — the worst case is your coins becoming worth ~zero, never negative. This bot is long-only with 1× leverage, so every account's worst case is its starting $1,000 going to near zero, never below.

2 · Candles & timeframes

The language every chart speaks.

Price history is chopped into candles. Each candle summarises one period — this bot uses 15-minute candles — with four numbers (OHLC): the price at the Open, the High and Low reached during the period, and the price at the Close. A green candle closed higher than it opened; a red one closed lower. The volume bar shows how much was traded.

Timeframe decides what each candle means. A 15m candle captures a quarter-hour; a daily candle captures a day. Signals that work on one timeframe may be noise on another — this whole experiment runs on 15m bars, one evaluation after every closed candle.

Why do the bot's candles look “delayed”?
Strategies only act on closed candles. A candle that is still forming can change completely in its last seconds, so trading it is gambling. The bot waits for the 15-minute candle to finish, then evaluates all 42 accounts against the finished bar. That's why updates arrive right after each quarter-hour.
What is a “wick”?
The thin line above/below a candle's body showing the high/low. A long upper wick means price shot up but was sold back down — buyers got rejected. Heikin-Ashi and engulfing strategies read wicks as rejection or acceptance stories.

3 · Trends & levels

The two big structures: direction and boundaries.

A trend is persistent direction. Uptrend = higher highs and higher lows; downtrend = lower lows and lower highs; everything else is a range (sideways). Roughly speaking: trend strategies make money in trends and bleed in ranges; mean-reversion strategies do the opposite. That's why this zoo of 42 accounts is full of both.

Support is a price floor where dips keep finding buyers; resistance is a ceiling where rallies keep getting sold. When price finally closes through a level, it often “flips” — broken resistance becomes support. Strategies either buy bounces off levels (pivot points, Fibonacci) or buy the breakout through them (Donchian, opening range).

What is a breakout exactly?
A candle that closes beyond a level or range extreme. Closes matter: a spike through a level that closes back inside is a fakeout. Multiple bot strategies (Donchian 20/10, opening range, volume-spike) wait for close-based breaks for exactly this reason.
What is a pullback?
A short counter-trend dip inside an uptrend — the market pausing to catch its breath. Dip-buying strategies (Connors RSI, VWAP, Fibonacci) specialise in buying pullbacks, ideally only when the longer trend is still up.

4 · Indicators 101

Math on price — and the #1 beginner misunderstanding.

An indicator is a calculation over recent prices (and sometimes volume) that summarises something: momentum (RSI, MACD), trend (moving averages), volatility (ATR, Bollinger), or flow (OBV, MFI). All of them are descriptions of the past, not predictions of the future. They help because markets have habits — stretched readings tend to revert, breaks of ranges tend to continue — but no indicator is ever “right” on its own.

Lag is the price of smoothing: indicators that ignore noise (SMA200, TRIX) react late but rarely lie; fast ones (RSI(2)) react early but cry wolf often. The best education is watching an indicator disagree with price — the Academy's glossary explains all 69 concepts used by the 42 strategies, with the formulas and the lessons that use them.

Why do different indicators disagree?
Because they measure different things on different windows. RSI might say “stretched down” while EMA says “trend still up” — both can be true. That disagreement is information; strategy 39 (consensus) makes five indicators vote and trades only majorities.

6 · How this bot actually works

The rulebook every account obeys — mirror of the Profile page.

The bot is an experiment: 42 isolated virtual accounts, each starting with $1,000 and each running one strategy on the same BTC/USDT 15-minute candle stream from Kraken. Every quarter-hour it fetches the latest closed candle, evaluates all 42 rulebooks, executes simulated fills at the candle close, deducts a 0.1% fee on both sides, and saves the state. No real orders are ever placed.

Key rules: long-only, 1× leverage, FIFO lot matching, minimum order $10, at most one fill per strategy per run per candle, an optional global stop-loss/take-profit overlay, and a warm-up per strategy (e.g. 200 candles for SMA200) during which an account simply waits. Each account is isolated — no shared capital, so one strategy blowing up costs only its own $1,000. The complete rulebook is rendered on the bot profile page straight from the bot's configuration.

Where does the data come from?
Public OHLCV candles from Kraken's API — no exchange keys, no accounts, no orders. The Market screen shows the very candle window the bot just evaluated, with indicator overlays computed in your browser.
Is the money real?
No. All balances are virtual and the whole state file is public in this repository. Consider every number a simulation result — useful for learning how strategies behave, useless as investment advice.

7 · Reading the results

How to judge a strategy honestly.
  • Equity vs Return. Equity = cash + BTC at the latest price. Return % compares it to the $1,000 start. Both move every candle (mark-to-market) even when nothing was traded.
  • Realized vs unrealized. Realized PnL comes from closed round trips; unrealized PnL is the open position's paper swing. Always look at both.
  • Win rate without context lies. A grid's 90% win rate with small wins can lose to a trend account's 30% win rate with big wins. Compare average win vs average loss too (that's the b in Kelly).
  • Warm-up matters. An account that waited 200 candles then traded well is behaving correctly, not “late”.
  • Drawdown is the truth. A +40% strategy that first fell −60% is a different animal from a steady +15%. Check max drawdown on the Portfolio screen.
  • Benchmark. BTC itself is the benchmark — a long-only strategy that underperforms buy-and-hold in a bull market is doing its job badly.
Why does every account start flat for a while?
Warm-up: each strategy refuses to signal until its indicators have enough history (up to 200 candles ≈ 2 days). During warm-up the account holds cash and its line sits at 0% — perfectly normal.

8 · Money & risk — the part that actually matters

Fees, sizing, and why some famous systems are traps.

Fees are a tax on ideas. At 0.1% per side, every round trip must beat 0.2% just to break even. A strategy that wins 55% of the time with 1:1 wins is a money-loser after fees; the same strategy with 1.5:1 wins is a business. Always subtract fees before believing a backtest.

Sizing decides survival. “How much to bet” matters more than “when to bet”. The bot shows three honest approaches: fixed fraction per entry (most accounts, 95% default), fixed schedules (DCA/grid per-leg sizes), and the Kelly criterion (strategy 38) — the mathematical link between your edge and your bet size, halved for safety because your edge estimate is never perfect.

Two classic traps to study, not copy: the martingale (strategy 36 doubles losing bets — it recovers fast and destroys accounts that remove its caps) and its mirror the anti-martingale (37, which adds to winners). The difference in their equity curves is the best risk-management lesson in this whole dashboard.

Why no leverage or shorting?
Leverage multiplies fees, liquidation risk and panic — it would contaminate the experiment. Long-only 1× keeps every account survivable and every curve comparable. If you can't make money without leverage, leverage won't fix that.
What is drawdown, really?
The fall from an equity peak to its trough. If your $1,000 becomes $600, you're in a 40% drawdown — and you now need +67% to recover. Drawdowns compound against you, which is why position sizing and stop rules exist.

9 · Words to know

The quick-reference glossary. Everything here has a fuller explanation in the indicator glossary.
Long / short
Long = bet price rises. Short = bet it falls. This bot is long-only.
Paper trading
Trading with simulated money against real market data.
Position
The coins an account currently holds. In position vs flat (all cash).
Lot
One buy = one lot. Accumulators hold ladders of lots bought at different prices.
Entry / exit
The signal that opens a position vs the one that closes it.
Stop-loss
A sell rule that caps loss when price moves against you.
Take-profit
A sell rule that locks in gains at a chosen profit level.
Trailing stop
A stop that follows price up and only triggers on a reversal of X%.
Volatility
How violently price moves — measured by ATR or standard deviation.
Liquidity
How easily you can trade without moving price. BTC/USDT is very liquid.
Slippage
The gap between expected and actual fill price.
Regime
The market's current character (trending / ranging, calm / volatile).
